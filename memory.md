# Memory

## AI tell-tales to avoid

Signs that give away AI-generated design/writing. Avoid all of these.

### Visual / design
- Don't spread info thinly across the whole page. Keep content grouped and focused, not scattered edge to edge.
- Avoid cream/off-white (#faf7f2 etc.) backgrounds by default.
- Don't reach for eyebrow labels by default — the little uppercase kicker above headings. Use only when it truly earns its place.
- Aggressive gradient backgrounds (purple-to-blue hero washes).
- Emoji as decoration — only if the brand actually uses them.
- Rounded containers with a left-border accent stripe ("callout card" cliché).
- Overused fonts: Inter, Roboto, Arial, and Fraunces.
- Glassmorphism, neon glows, and drop-shadow overload.
- Drawing imagery with SVG instead of using real photos/placeholders.
- Perfectly even, characterless grids with no rhythm or focal point.
- Inventing brand-new colors instead of using the brand/design system.
- Center-everything layouts with no intentional hierarchy.
- Decorative icons and stat badges that carry no real information.

### Writing / copy
- Don't be salesy. Keep copy plain and matter-of-fact, not pitchy.
- No sleazy, gimmicky, punchline-style copy. Avoid the setup-then-zinger "rap" cadence. Just say the thing.
- Avoid the "jump twice, then dance" tri-colon cadence: two short parallel beats followed by a payoff clause. It reads as a formula.
- Avoid contrast phrasing ("not this, but that" / "it's not X, it's Y").
- Avoid the two-sentence setup-then-payoff cadence ("They already know X. What they need now is Y."). Write it as one plain flowing sentence.
- Avoid em dashes.
- Filler and padding: sections that exist to fill space, not to say something.
- Data slop: made-up numbers, percentages, and stats with no source.
- "In today's fast-paced world…" and similar throat-clearing openers.
- Overuse of "elevate," "unlock," "leverage," "seamless," "robust," "delve," "supercharge," "game-changer."
- Empty superlatives and hype with nothing concrete behind them.
- Identical sentence rhythm throughout.
- Rule-of-three everywhere ("faster, smarter, better").
- Hedging everything; never taking a clear position.
- Summaries that restate the intro instead of adding anything.

## Presentation conventions

- Name the document for what it actually is up front (e.g. "90-Day Growth Plan," not a vague "Memo" or "Report"). The reader should know the genre from the cover.
- Give content slides proper narrative section titles that mark their role in the argument — The Problem, The Insight, The Solution / The Plan, The Scoreboard, The Recommendation — instead of generic or purely descriptive labels.
- These narrative titles are the exception to the "no eyebrow kickers" rule: they earn their place because they carry the story's structure. Keep them consistent in style across slides.

## Decks specifically

- The document's title is a plain, conversational name for what it is — "The 90-Day Growth Plan," "Onboarding Report," "Brand Manual." Never a clever/zinger cover headline.
- No zingers anywhere on the deck. Slide headlines are plain statements, not punchlines, wordplay, or setup-payoff lines.
- Speaker notes are read aloud — write them as a spoken script, not a caption. Use contractions, "we / our / you," soft hedges ("we should," "if," "a bit," "properly"), and natural spoken rhythm. "These are the four numbers we should watch to know if our plan's working" beats the clipped "These are the four numbers we watch to know the plan is working."
- Speaker notes are written as full, flowing sentences — real paragraphs.

## Rules of thumb
- Less is more. One thousand no's for every yes.
- Every element must earn its place — cut anything that doesn't.
- Take a clear position; say the honest thing.
- Use real brand colors/type/voice, not generic defaults.
- Vary rhythm, layout, and sentence length on purpose.
